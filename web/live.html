      Step into the lab

      Your camera finds 33 points on your body. They drive a full OpenSim skeleton with 88 leg muscles, and a physics solver estimates your joint loads and which muscles are working, every frame. Nothing leaves this device.

      • Stand 2 to 3 m from the camera with your whole body in view, in good light.
      • Turn side-on for squats and lunges: knee and hip angles are most accurate from the side.
      • Hold still for 3 seconds at the start: the twin calibrates to your size.

      Method & limits

      Pipeline, every frame

      1. Tracking. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker finds 33 body points in 3D from one camera, on this device. Each point is smoothed with a One Euro filter.
      2. Skeleton. Each bone of the Lenhart 2015 OpenSim full-body model is placed by aligning its real body frame to the tracked points, chained joint to joint with lengths fixed at calibration (scaled to your height).
      3. Joint angles follow OpenSim's conventions (hip flexion, adduction, rotation; knee flexion; ankle dorsiflexion).
      4. Ground force. The camera has no force plate, so the total ground force is computed from Newton's second law over all body segments (mass from your body weight), and shared between the feet by where the centre of pressure falls.
      5. Joint moments come from 3D Newton-Euler inverse dynamics up each leg (foot, shank, thigh).
      6. Muscle effort. Static optimisation shares each leg's joint moments across its 44 muscles, minimising the sum of squared activations. Moment arms come from OpenSim; the quadriceps' leverage through the kneecap comes from a solved OpenSim JAM knee-bending simulation (0 to 130 degrees). Each muscle's capacity follows its force-length curve.

      What this cannot do

      • One camera sees depth poorly. Movements along the camera's line of sight are the least accurate; side-on views suit squats and lunges best.
      • Forces are estimated, not measured. Ground forces and muscle activations are model outputs. Fast movements (landings, sprints) amplify tracking noise in the accelerations.
      • The muscle-sharing rule is a model choice. Minimising squared activation (OpenSim's default) gives stronger muscles proportionally more load, so in deep squats the large vasti can read near their maximum before smaller synergists do.
      • A generic body. Bones and muscles are one adult model scaled to your height and weight; muscles are only modelled for the legs (the arms and trunk carry no muscles here).
      • Not medical advice. Coaching cues describe movement; they do not diagnose or prescribe.
