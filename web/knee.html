What happened in this knee

Four ligaments hold the shin bone under the thigh bone. This injury took out three of them.

ACL · tornRuns diagonally inside the joint and stops the shin sliding forward. In the Lachman test the healthy knee moves a few millimetres; without the ACL the shin slides much further, and already sits forward at rest.
PCL · tornThe thicker cruciate behind it, stopping the shin sagging backward. The posterior drawer test pushes the bent knee back: the injured knee gives several times more.
LCL · tornOn the outer side, from thigh bone to fibula. It keeps the outer joint closed when the knee is bent inward (varus). Without it the outer side opens about three times as far.
MCL · stretchedOn the inner side. Still there but looser, it now carries far more than its share in every test, because the ligaments that used to help are gone.

What to look for

  • Colour is strain: grey bundles are slack, gold to red bundles are pulled tight. Watch which ones light up in each test.
  • Red stumps are the torn ligaments at their real attachment points: they carry nothing.
  • The numbers on the right compare both knees against the healthy knee at rest.
  • Hover a ligament (or a name in the list) to see its strain and force.

Please read this

This is a generic adult knee injured to match the description, used to understand the mechanics. It is not anyone's own knee, it is not a diagnosis, and it says nothing about how a particular person will heal or recover. Treatment and rehab decisions belong to the surgeon and physiotherapist.

Method & limits

Model

The Lenhart et al. 2015 knee (OpenSim JAM, Apache 2.0): a 6-degree-of-freedom tibiofemoral and patellofemoral joint built from MRI, 76 ligament bundles (Blankevoort 1991 model, wrapping around the bones), elastic-foundation cartilage contact, and the lower-limb muscles held at minimal activation.

Simulations

Forward simulations with the JAM forsim tool: the knee settles straight, bends to the test angle, holds, then a load ramps on over one second (Lachman 100 N forward at 25°, posterior drawer 100 N back at 90°, varus / valgus 10 N·m at 20°; bending 0→90° with no load). Our healthy Lachman reproduces the published OpenSim JAM example exactly (5.9 mm under 100 N).

Injury

Grade 3 = complete tear, modelled as removing every ACL (12), PCL (10) and LCL (4) bundle. The stretched MCL is an illustrative assumption: superficial and deep MCL bundles 6% longer before they tighten and half as stiff.

Limits

  • No menisci in this model, and the muscles are kept nearly relaxed. Real people tense up to protect an injured knee, so the injured knee's millimetres here are larger than a real knee would show. Read the pattern, not the exact numbers.
  • One generic knee geometry. Ligament properties vary between people.
  • Mechanics only: nothing about healing, surgery or recovery time.
